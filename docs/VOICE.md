# Writing these guides

Write as an engineer explaining a piece of work to another person.

- Begin with the task or example. Explain the terms when the reader needs them.
- Prefer concrete verbs: read, call, retry, stop, check. Use technical terms when they add precision.
- Explain a decision and its consequence together. A feature list rarely explains why a choice matters.
- Use tables for real comparisons and lists for steps. Let the rest read as connected paragraphs.
- Avoid repeating the same disclaimer after every paragraph. Put each limitation near the claim it qualifies and keep detailed records in evidence/.
- First-person preferences must reflect the owner's stated approach. Do not invent client stories, mistakes, personal anecdotes, measured results or testimonials.
- Keep commands, identifiers, sources and implementation status exact. A friendlier sentence must not strengthen a claim.
- English is primary. Translations should preserve meaning without copying awkward sentence structures.

The aim is clearer writing with a recognizable point of view. No claim is made about passing an AI detector or concealing how the material was produced.
